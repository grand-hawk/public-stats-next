**Line-of-sight thickness** is the armour a round really has to get through once the angle of the hit is counted. The steeper the hit, the more armour is in the way.

## How much the angle adds

| Angle of the hit | A 100 mm plate counts as |
| ---------------- | ------------------------ |
| 0° (flat on)     | 100 mm                   |
| 30°              | 115 mm                   |
| 45°              | 141 mm                   |
| 60°              | 200 mm                   |
| 70°              | 292 mm                   |

The plate's thickness is divided by the cosine of the impact angle. Its <Term id="ke" /> and any <Term id="ce" /> are added together first.

This is why you angle your hull, and why you aim for the flattest plate you can see.

A steep hit also takes away some of the round's own penetration. See the <WikiLink to="penetration#angle-penalty">angle penalty</WikiLink> and <WikiLink to="penetration#overmatch">overmatch</WikiLink>.

## Parts that ignore the angle

- **Engines, fuel tanks, transmissions and batteries** are equally tough from every direction.
- **Gun breeches** never count as more than 100 mm.
- **Composite armour** is measured a different way. See below.

## Composite armour

Composite armour is measured by how far the round travels through the block, and not by a flat thickness. The distance travelled inside the block is multiplied by the block's composite value. A hit at an angle, or along the length of the block, meets more protection.

Composite blocks can have extra <Term id="ce" /> on top. Top-attack EFP warheads ignore that bonus.
