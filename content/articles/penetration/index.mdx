**Penetration** is how much armour a round can get through. The figure on a [shell page](/shells) is the best case: point blank against a flat plate. This page covers what takes away from it.

## Distance

Only AP and APHE lose penetration as they fly. APFSDS, HEAT, HE and missiles keep all of it at any range.

| Range   | Penetration an AP shell keeps |
| ------- | ----------------------------- |
| 500 m   | 98%                           |
| 1,000 m | 95%                           |
| 1,500 m | 93%                           |
| 2,000 m | 91%                           |

The range counts the whole flight, including any <WikiLink to="ricochet">ricochet</WikiLink> on the way.

## Angle penalty

A steep hit makes the armour thicker (see <WikiLink to="line-of-sight-thickness" />). It also costs the round some of its own penetration. The penalty stops getting worse past 75°.

Long-rod APFSDS is the exception. It handles sloped armour much better than a full-calibre shell.

## Overmatch

When a full-calibre AP or APHE shell is wider than the plate is thick, the plate's slope stops mattering much, and the shell loses far less penetration to the angle than it normally would. For example, a 122 mm shell hitting a 30 mm roof loses little to the angle.

Only full-calibre AP and APHE shells can overmatch.

## Going through more than one plate

A round keeps whatever penetration the plate did not use up. For example, a shell with 300 mm that goes through 200 mm of armour has 100 mm left for the next thing in its path.

If the plate took more than 75% of its penetration, the round slows down as well.

## Walls and terrain

Walls, buildings and terrain count as armour: 30 mm for every metre of material. Only AP and APHE can go through them. Every other round stops at the first wall.
